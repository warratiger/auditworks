Approach

Four moves from a question to a defensible answer.

The same loop runs whichever decision you are facing, so you learn it once and reuse it.

Try a decision tree
  1. 01

    Name the decision

    Start from what you have to decide: whether to take the engagement, what to do about a difference, whether to rely on a control. Each has its own tree.

  2. 02

    Answer one question at a time

    Questions are short and answerable from the file in front of you. Each one carries the reason it is asked, so you can test it against the facts.

  3. 03

    Read the outcome and its paragraph

    An outcome says proceed, wait, stop or focus here, and points to the paragraphs of the standards it rests on, so you can check the wording yourself.

  4. 04

    Follow the connections

    Every outcome opens the topics it depends on in the map. The next decision then starts from what this one established.

About

The method.

Aequon turns the standards on auditing into questions you can answer on an engagement, and keeps the reasons next to the answers.

Manifesto

A standard tells you what is required. It rarely tells you why, and it never tells you which question to ask first. Aequon starts from the problem each rule was written to solve, then attaches the technical wording once the idea is clear.

Every topic in the map is chained to the one before it. A topic that arrives without a reason is a fact to memorise. A topic that answers a question the last one left open is something you can reconstruct on your own.

Knowing the concepts and making the decision are different skills. The decision trees put the concepts to work on one scenario, one question at a time, with the reason for each question one tap away.

Aequon supports judgment and does not replace it. Outcomes cite the paragraphs they rest on so you can check them against the standard and your firm's methodology, and then record your own conclusions on the file.
